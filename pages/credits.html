Image and film credits

The gallery combines original astrophotography, rendered AstroWeb films, published mission images and recordings of the English web and TV interfaces. Processing includes resizing, cropping, camera animation and overlays.

The current TV viewer and menu screenshots were recorded with AstroWeb TV 0.8.0 and the AstroWeb 1.5.2 interface. They use the real demonstration collection and were recorded in the Google TV emulator, cropped to the application display.

Original captures and processing

Ron Hoebe: IC 1396, NGC 7000, NGC 6992, M31 and the visible-light Sun. The Andromeda layer comparison uses aligned exports from AstroWizard. The AstroWeb software is MIT-licensed. The software licence applies to the code.

New exploration views

The English viewer and calendar screenshots show real AstroWeb captures by Ron Hoebe. Stars in 3D uses Gaia DR3 distances and crops from the original stellar layer; the logarithmic distance layout is a diagram. Light journey uses documented target distances or NED redshifts and Astropy Planck18. Mosaics reproject original captures with Reproject. Calendar calculations use Astroplan, Astropy and ERFA.

Solar comparison uses NASA/SDO HMI and AIA observations through Helioviewer, with dated FITS metadata and SunPy coordinates. Archive searches can use ESA/Euclid VIS public products and NASA/ESA/CSA JWST calibrated images. Provider, instrument and filter credits accompany each available destination; an enabled search does not imply coverage of every object.

Lunar detail journeys

The lunar journeys use NASA SVS Dial-A-Moon (Ernie Wright) for the dated orientation reference and LROC WAC and Apollo NAC mosaics (NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University) for surface imagery. The landing sites use NASA NSSDCA coordinates. Craters and seas use the IAU/USGS Gazetteer.

Nightscape examples

  • Star trails over La Silla, G. Lambert/ESO. A milky arc over Paranal, ESO/G. Brammer. Both under CC BY 4.0; used as single-image pan/zoom examples.

Gaia, maps, typography and source licences

Gaia parallax films use Gaia DR3 / ESA / AIP. Matched stars use high signal-to-noise parallax distance estimates. Additional local interpolation and amplified camera travel are artistic. The examples include IC 1396, M31, NGC 6992 and the 9 November 2026 NGC 7000 capture.

Current face-on Galaxy map and map-animation example: ESA/Gaia/DPAC, Stefan Payne-Wardenaar, CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO. Resized, converted, animated and overlaid with catalogue labels under the same map licence. The published English overview includes the earlier NASA/JPL-Caltech/R. Hurt (SSC/Caltech) reconstruction.
