## Aanvullende kaartlabels / Additional atlas labels

The local atlas-landmarks.json file contains separate attributed catalogue subsets. Coordinates are in decimal degrees, and available heliocentric distances are converted from parsecs or kiloparsecs to approximate light-years. Missing or placeholder distances are excluded from the face-on map. These data retain their source terms separately from the application code.

- **HYG 4.1** (source S), David Nash / Astronexus. Named objects, coordinates and valid distances from [HYG Database](https://github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database/tree/main/hyg/CURRENT). Extracted, rounded and reformatted under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).
- **OpenNGC** (source O and field_objects), Mattia Verga and contributors. Coordinates, designations and common names extracted from the NGC and addendum tables. Filtered, reformatted and partly translated under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). [Original dataset and notices](https://github.com/mattiaverga/OpenNGC).
- **Milky Way globular clusters** (source H), William E. Harris (1996, AJ, 112, 1487; December 2010 edition). Positions and heliocentric distances in Part I of the [original catalogue](https://physics.mcmaster.ca/~harris/mwgc.dat). The catalogue is supplied free of charge; redistribution must refer to that original source and must be free of charge. [HEASARC documentation](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/all/globclust.html).
- **CDS Sesame/SIMBAD** (source C). Additional local nebula, cluster and stellar positions resolved by their catalogue identifiers using [CDS Sesame](https://cds.unistra.fr/cgi-bin/Sesame). Where a NASA distance is supplied separately, its URL is recorded in the object entry.

**M31 / NGC 224** — [NASA · Messier 31](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/hubble-messier-catalog/messier-31/) en [ESA · M31 the Andromeda Galaxy](https://www.esa.int/media/m31-the-andromeda-galaxy). Eigen tweetalige samenvatting / original bilingual summary. The discovery overlay also uses the OpenNGC object classifications under the same catalogue licence cited above.

**AllWISE** — [IRSA AllWISE colour HiPS, survey properties and full acknowledgements](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/hips/CDS/AllWISE/RGB-W4-W2-W1/properties). Red = W4 (22 μm), green = W2 (4.6 μm), blue = W1 (3.4 μm). These data products are from WISE (UCLA and JPL/Caltech) and NEOWISE (JPL/Caltech), funded by NASA. HiPS colour tiles from CDS via IRSA, reprojected to the capture for display. Reported arcseconds per pixel refer to the display mosaic sampling at the top edge; survey angular resolution depends on band.
**Galaxy environment** — OpenNGC positions and object classes as credited above. Confirmed associations and selected distances from the NASA Hubble Messier catalogue: [M32](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/hubble-messier-catalog/messier-32/), [M110](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/hubble-messier-catalog/messier-110/) and [M51](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/hubble-messier-catalog/messier-51/). Further object research via NASA/IPAC NED. Catalogue positions alone do not establish physical membership.

The ESO panorama is registered to Galactic coordinates by fitting a rigid three-axis rotation to stellar peaks in the bundled image against the Hipparcos catalogue. A separate set of 104 bright reference stars at |b| < 60° had a median peak residual of 1.8 pixels in the 6000 × 3000 master. This is a check of the photographic background, separate from the Gaia solution of each uploaded photograph. De Poolkaart gebruikt dezelfde Hipparcos-sterren en Stellarium-lijnen als de Skykaart, geprojecteerd rond de noordelijke of zuidelijke hemelpool.

**M31 sterrenvelden / Hubble star fields** — [ESA/Hubble heic2501a](https://esahubble.org/images/heic2501a/), NASA, ESA, B. Williams (University of Washington). PHAT + PHAST optical mosaic, F475W and F814W. AstroWeb resamples and animates windows of the published 42208 × 9870 Zoomify raster using the release’s AVM TAN astrometry, with coordinates referred to the original capture. Tiles and derived movies are cached in the data directory. [Image use and credit](https://esahubble.org/copyright/).

**Sterrentijdmachine / Stellar time machine** — derived from the [HYG 4.1 catalogue and field definitions](https://github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database/tree/main/hyg), David Nash / Astronexus, CC BY-SA 4.0. stellar-motion.json is the subset of heliocentric Cartesian positions (pc) and velocities (pc/year) for the plotted Hipparcos stars with usable distances. It retains the [CC BY-SA 4.0 licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The simulation uses the catalogue’s velocity vectors, including radial motion where available, and extrapolates linearly from J2000. No Galactic acceleration, future luminosity, Earth precession or constellation boundary evolution is modelled.

**Additional Hubble journeys** — based on observations made with the NASA/ESA Hubble Space Telescope, obtained from the [ESA Hubble Science Archive](https://hst.esac.esa.int/ehst/) and [Hubble Legacy Archive](https://hla.stsci.edu/). The displayed scientific intensity image is cropped, stretched and rendered as a monochrome zoom. PHAT/PHAST M31 flights retain their separate colour mosaic credit above.

**Automatic Hubble archive destinations** — [NASA/STScI MAST](https://mast.stsci.edu/portal/Mashup/Clients/Mast/Portal.html) and the [ESA Hubble Science Archive](https://hst.esac.esa.int/ehst/) supply target names and observation polygons. **Additional archive journeys / aanvullende archiefreizen** — [Astroquery](https://astroquery.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) (BSD-3-Clause) provides the TAP client for [ESA Euclid](https://euclid.esac.esa.int/) VIS mosaics from DR1 and Q1 and the [ESA JWST archive](https://jwst.esac.esa.int/archive/). Euclid science cutouts are credited to ESA / Euclid / Euclid Consortium. Public calibrated JWST imaging is credited to NASA / ESA / CSA, with the instrument and filter alongside each destination; [MAST](https://mast.stsci.edu/) supplies fallback catalogue access. Single-filter scientific data are displayed as stretched grayscale images, not as official colour publicity releases. WCS registration, cuts and movies are derived locally and do not replace the source data. Publication or scientific reuse should follow each mission's data acknowledgement and citation guidance.

**Observatory mosaics and stellar space views** — [Reproject](https://reproject.readthedocs.io/en/stable/) and Astropy WCS reproject user-owned processed rasters, including their actual orientation and reflection. The visual mosaic applies feathering and bounded brightness matching; it is not a photometric stack. The 3D view uses cropped pixels from the user's own star layer with the Gaia DR3/AIP catalogue and quality cuts credited above. No additional stars or interpolated stellar distances are introduced. Camera movement and the nebula image plane are visualization choices.

The distance map retains angular positions within the capture and uses a logarithmic distance axis over the displayed range. Its 3D display coordinates are a diagram, not a true-scale Cartesian reconstruction of the nebula or an inferred gas volume. The light journey is a schematic path with catalogue/cosmological travel times.

**Cosmic light-travel time and observing calendar** — [Astropy cosmology, Planck18](https://docs.astropy.org/en/stable/cosmology/realizations.html), based on the Planck 2018 cosmological results. [NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/) supplies optional catalogue redshifts via Astroquery; each result retains its object source link. Published nearby distances keep their original credits. [Astroplan](https://astroplan.readthedocs.io/en/stable/) and Astropy/ERFA calculate the observing windows and lunar conditions without weather or a terrain model.

**Dated solar comparison** — NASA / Solar Dynamics Observatory / HMI and AIA, distributed by the [Helioviewer API](https://api.helioviewer.org/docs/v2/api/index.html). The display images use Helioviewer's published instrument colour tables. [SunPy](https://sunpy.org/) uses the source FITS calibration to calculate heliographic coordinates and Howard differential-rotation predictions. AIA 171 Å is extreme ultraviolet shown in display colours, not visible-light colour. An operator-selected surface coordinate is propagated; no sunspot identification or unmeasured orientation of the user's photograph is claimed.

**Registered lunar detail flights** — NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University, [LROC WAC global mosaic and Apollo NAC mosaics](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/), distributed as georeferenced imagery by [Lunaserv](https://lunaserv.im-ldi.com/about.html). [NASA SVS Dial-A-Moon](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/help/) (Ernie Wright) supplies the hourly phase, libration and orientation reference. Registration uses measured surface landmarks, including held-out checks, rather than an assumed camera orientation. Apollo coordinates follow [NASA NSSDCA](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/lunar_sites.html); crater and mare coordinates follow the [IAU/USGS Gazetteer](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/). Map tiles at different resolutions share lunar coordinates throughout the flight. The source mosaics were acquired at other times and lighting conditions; changing shadows and colours are not a same-night observation. Small relief and topocentric differences can remain at the initial full-disk registration. Crater/mare trips use the global 100 m/pixel WAC data; the Apollo areas add higher-resolution NAC mosaics. Raw publicity photographs with unknown projection, especially oblique summit views, are not used as registered map layers.

**Saturn detail journeys** — NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute. [Bursting at the Seams, PIA11688](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/bursting-at-the-seams/) shows the ice plumes of Enceladus in a separate observation.
